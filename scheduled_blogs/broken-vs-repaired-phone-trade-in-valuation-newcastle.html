1. How Carrier and Buyback Grading Algorithms Penalise Minor Damage

Most major Australian telco and manufacturer trade-in portals divide incoming handsets into just two or three broad buckets: "Good / Working Condition" and "Damaged / Non-Working". There is rarely a proportional middle ground for a small fault.

For example, if your phone's OLED panel, touch digitiser, Face ID, and cameras all work flawlessly, but there is a 10-millimetre hairline chip in the top corner of the glass—or a cracked rear camera sapphire lens ring—the automated assessment immediately drops the device into the "Damaged" bucket. That single classification shift routinely wipes out the majority of the handset's trade-in credit, even when the actual bench repair would take 30 minutes.

2. Warning: Why Third-Party Screens Can Trigger Automated Portal Rejections

Before paying to fix a screen purely to submit the phone to an official manufacturer mail-in trade-in portal, there is an important technical catch you need to know. On recent Apple models (iPhone 11 and newer), if a standard aftermarket screen is installed without OEM serial pairing, iOS displays an "Unknown Part" or "Display message" in Settings > General > About.

Some automated mail-in buyback warehouses treat an iOS "Unknown Part" notification as a grading deduction, whereas private local buyers in Newcastle (on Facebook Marketplace or Gumtree) care far more about having a crisp, uncracked, fully responsive display with working True Tone and a 90-day shop warranty receipt in hand.
